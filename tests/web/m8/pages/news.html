Screen readers learn to keep up with the modern web

A new generation of assistive technology promises faster, steadier reading of complex pages.

By Ada Lovelace, Technology editor

14 October 2026, 09:30 · 6 min read

  • Share by email
  • Copy link

When a page loads thousands of nodes, a screen reader has to decide quickly what to say first. Researchers measured the delay on a range of sites and found large differences.

The latency problem

The biggest complaint is lag. Users who navigate by heading expect the next heading to be spoken at once, not after a pause that grows with the size of the document.

Every keystroke should feel instant, whatever is on the page.

A user, speaking to the paper

Developers can help: landmarks, headings and labelled controls make a page easier to scan. See the official guidance and the test suite.

What changed

  1. Browse mode keeps a mirror of the page in memory.
  2. Navigation commands answer from the mirror without a round trip.
  3. Live regions are throttled so a ticker cannot drown the speech.
